Buying property in Morocco from abroad

Buying in Morocco without living there is common and well organised. The difficulty is not legal, it is logistical: preparing the right documents, in the right order, and securing the money trail.

Who can buy

Buying property in Morocco is open to Moroccans living abroad and to non-resident foreigners alike. One important restriction applies to agricultural land, whose acquisition follows particular rules. For an apartment or a house in an urban area, no such restriction applies.

The decisive point: convertibility

This is the most important mechanism in this guide, and the one most often discovered too late.

If you finance your purchase with foreign currency transferred from abroad through the official banking channel, you keep the right to repatriate the proceeds of a later resale and the rental income, in the original currency. If you pay with funds already in Morocco in non-convertible dirhams, that right is not acquired.

In other words: how you pay today determines whether you can take your money out tomorrow.

In practice this means opening a suitable account with a Moroccan bank — a convertible dirham or foreign currency account — and routing the funds through it, keeping every transfer advice and bank certificate.

Keep every transfer document in a dedicated file, on paper and digitally. You will be asked for them years later, when you sell.

Buying without travelling: power of attorney

You do not have to be present at every stage. A power of attorney allows a trusted person to sign on your behalf.

The power of attorney must be:

  • drawn up before a notary, or at the Moroccan consulate in your country of residence;
  • translated and legalised if drafted abroad;
  • specific — naming the property, setting a maximum price and listing exactly which acts are authorised.

An overly broad power of attorney is a real risk. One limited to an identified transaction is not.

Documents to prepare

  • Valid passport or national identity card.
  • Proof of address in your country of residence.
  • Proof of income or tax assessment, if you borrow.
  • Statements of the account the funds come from.
  • Power of attorney, if you are not signing in person.

Allow generous timeframes: sworn translation and legalisation often take longer than the purchase itself.

Financing from abroad

Moroccan banks offer mortgages to non-residents, generally on more demanding terms than for residents: a higher deposit, sometimes a shorter term, and foreign income documents to provide. Some banks have desks dedicated to Moroccans abroad, in France, Spain, Belgium or the Netherlands, which greatly simplifies the arrangement.

After the purchase: managing remotely

An empty property 2,000 kilometres away deteriorates and costs money. Plan from the outset for:

  • building management: who pays the charges, how, and who you deal with;
  • letting, if that is your plan, and its taxation;
  • a trusted local representative for emergencies and handing over keys;
  • taxation in your country of residence: a property in Morocco may have to be declared there. A tax treaty generally avoids double taxation, but it does not remove the duty to declare.
