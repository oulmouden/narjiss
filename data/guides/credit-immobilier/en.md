## The only figure that matters: the monthly payment

Moroccan banks think in terms of **debt-to-income ratio**: the share of your net monthly income taken up by the repayment, including all other credit commitments — car loans and consumer credit included.

Above a certain threshold, the application is refused whatever your arguments. Below it, everything else becomes negotiable.

!> Have at least two banks confirm the maximum debt-to-income ratio actually applied, and state whether it varies with income level. Publish nothing before that.

The practical consequence is simple: **clear your small loans before submitting a mortgage application**. A consumer loan ending in eight months can on its own tip a file the wrong way.

## The deposit

Banks rarely finance the whole amount. They expect a deposit, and above all they expect it to be **traceable**: savings built up regularly are far more reassuring than a sum that appeared the month before.

Beware a frequent confusion: the deposit tops up the price of the property, **but deed costs are paid on top**, and rarely on credit. A buyer with exactly the required deposit and nothing else ends up stuck at signature.

!> Confirm the minimum deposit usually required, depending on whether the buyer is resident or non-resident, and on the type of property (new-build, existing, land).

## Documents to provide

Prepare the complete file before the first meeting. A complete file gets processed; an incomplete one waits.

**For everyone**

- Copy of national identity card or passport.
- Recent proof of address.
- Bank statements for recent months — the bank reads your habits there, not just your balance.
- Preliminary agreement, reservation contract or sale undertaking.

**Employees**

- Employment and salary certificate.
- Recent payslips.
- Social security fund certificate.

**Self-employed, traders, professionals**

- Accounts and tax returns for recent financial years.
- Business account statements.
- Trade register entry or equivalent.

> One rule applies to everyone: in the three months before submitting, keep your accounts impeccable. No missed payments, no overdraft. The bank reads those statements line by line.

## Fixed or variable rate

A **fixed rate** does not move for the whole term: your repayment is known to the last day. A **variable rate** follows a reference index; it often starts lower, but your repayment can rise.

For a first purchase, over a long term, the predictability of a fixed rate has real value — especially if your budget is tight. A variable rate makes sense over short terms or when an early resale is envisaged.

Whatever you choose, compare offers on the **total cost of the credit**, not on the headline rate: arrangement fees, insurance and early repayment terms often weigh more than a few hundredths of a point.

## Insurance and guarantees

**Death and disability insurance** is obligatory in practice. Its cost depends on your age and health, and it is not marginal over the full term. It may involve a medical questionnaire, or even examinations.

The bank also takes a **mortgage charge** on the property, registered at the land registry: that is its security in case of default. The related costs are borne by you.

!> Have the bank specify: average insurance cost by age, whether it can be taken out elsewhere, mortgage registration fees, early repayment penalties.

## How long it takes

Allow several weeks between submission and release of funds, longer for a non-resident or a self-employed applicant. The stages: assessment of the file, agreement in principle, valuation of the property, written loan offer, then release — in one go for a completed property, in instalments for an off-plan purchase.

!> Confirm the average timeframes observed, from submission to agreement and then to release.

## Five costly mistakes

1. **Approaching only one bank.** The differences between institutions are real, on rates as much as on fees.
2. **Negotiating after the agreement.** Everything is negotiated before: arrangement fees, insurance, early repayment terms.
3. **Forgetting deed costs** in the financing plan.
4. **Changing job** while the application is being assessed.
5. **Borrowing to the maximum of your capacity.** A budget calculated to the last dirham does not survive the first surprise.
