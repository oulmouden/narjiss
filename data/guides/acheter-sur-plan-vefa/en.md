## What off-plan purchase means

Buying off-plan — sale in a future state of completion, known in Morocco by its French acronym VEFA — means buying a home before or during its construction. You become the owner as the work progresses, and you pay the same way, in instalments tied to the real state of the site.

This mechanism is governed by Moroccan law. It is not a private arrangement between you and a developer: the contract follows precise rules, and that is exactly what protects the buyer.

!> Have the notary confirm the exact reference of the legislation applicable to VEFA and its latest amendments before this page is published.

## Why buy off-plan

- **The price.** A home bought at the start of marketing is generally cheaper than at handover.
- **The choice.** Early buyers choose the floor, the orientation and the view. Late buyers take what is left.
- **The staging.** You pay at the pace of the site rather than all at once, which eases the cash effort.
- **New-build.** No remedial works, recent standards, and construction guarantees running from handover.

The trade-off is real: you wait, and you buy on the strength of a plan and of the developer's reputation.

## The steps, in order

1. **The reservation contract.** It identifies the unit, sets the price and the expected delivery date, and provides for the deposit. Read the refund conditions of that deposit: it is the clause that matters.
2. **Securing the financing**, if you borrow. The reservation contract must state what happens if the bank refuses.
3. **The preliminary sale contract**, signed before a notary or adoul, which organises the staged payments.
4. **The stage payments**, triggered by real construction progress, certified by a qualified professional.
5. **Handover**, with an inspection visit and a written report.
6. **The final deed**, transferring full ownership once the building is complete.

> Never pay funds outside the framework set out in the contract, and insist on a receipt in your name for every payment. A serious developer has no reason to refuse.

## What the law guarantees

The VEFA framework provides several protections for the buyer: a compulsory written contract, payments staged against progress, the involvement of a notary or adoul, and construction guarantees after handover.

!> To be detailed here, after validation by the notary: the guarantee required of the developer, the legal ceiling on payments before completion, the duration of post-handover guarantees, and remedies in case of delay. No figure or duration should be published before that validation.

## Five things to check before signing

**The land title.** Ask for a recent copy. The land must be registered, and the developer must own it or hold a right allowing them to build.

**The building permit.** It exists, it is dated, and it matches the project being sold to you — number of floors included.

**The delivery date and what happens if it is missed.** A contract that announces a date without providing any consequence for delay does not protect you.

**The descriptive specification.** This is the document that binds the developer on materials and equipment. "Quality finishes" commits to nothing; a detailed specification does.

**The developer's previous deliveries.** Go and see a building they handed over three years ago, and talk to the residents. No sales pitch is worth that visit.

## At handover

The inspection visit is not a formality. Take your time, come with a professional if you can, and record **in writing** every reservation in the handover report: an unwritten reservation does not exist. Check conformity with the descriptive specification, that equipment works, the surface areas, and the state of the common areas.
