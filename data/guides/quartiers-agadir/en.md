## Start with the question that decides everything

Before comparing areas, answer a single question: **what is this home for?** The three possible answers do not lead to the same place.

- **Living there day to day.** What matters: the commute at peak hours, schools, everyday shops, quiet in the evening.
- **Investing to let.** What matters: real rental demand, the tenant profile, yield relative to price, how easily it re-lets.
- **A second home or retirement.** What matters: proximity to the sea, quiet, healthcare, and how easy it is to leave the property closed for months.

The same apartment can be an excellent investment and a poor place for you to live. Both judgements are valid; they are not the same judgement.

## Six criteria to measure on site

**The real journey, not the distance.** Five kilometres in Agadir is not five kilometres elsewhere. Make the journey yourself, on a weekday, at the hour you would actually make it.

**The noise, at the right hour.** An area that is quiet at 11 in the morning can be noisy in the evening or at weekends. Go back at least twice, once of them after dark.

**What is being built around it.** Look at the neighbouring empty plots and ask what is permitted there. A clear view today can disappear in two years, and that changes the value of your property.

**Everyday services within walking distance.** Bakery, pharmacy, school, transport: these are what make an area liveable, and what your future tenants are looking for.

**Aspect and orientation.** On the coast, orientation determines light, summer heat and exposure to wind. The same apartment, two orientations, two very different levels of comfort.

**The state of the common areas in neighbouring buildings.** It is the best indicator of the quality of building management and of how the area will hold up in ten years.

## Living there: the most common mistake

Choosing on price per square metre alone. A cheaper home whose daily commute takes forty minutes longer costs far more over ten years than the saving made — in fuel, in wear on the vehicle, and in hours of your life.

Do the full calculation before deciding: price, charges, commute, and what the area will save or cost you day to day.

## Investing: look at demand, not the advertised yield

A theoretical yield is calculated on a hoped-for rent. A real yield is calculated on rent received, less void months, less non-recoverable charges, less maintenance.

The right questions to ask before buying to let:

- Who are the tenants in this area, and are they stable?
- How long does a comparable property take to re-let?
- How many new apartments will reach the market here in the next two years?

!> Have the sales team provide observed data: average re-letting time by area, observed rent ranges by unit type. Without that data, this section stays general.
